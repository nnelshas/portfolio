Resume

I’m a Robotics PhD candidate at Georgia Tech (NSF-GRFP Fellow, graduating May 2027). I build real-time sensing and control systems on real hardware for noisy, non-linear systems, combining control theory, physical sensors, and machine learning. My thesis unites all three to monitor and control metal 3D printing as it happens; in parallel, I work with Los Alamos National Laboratory on perception for robots that inspect structures for hidden damage. I’m drawn to safety-critical autonomy: self-driving vehicles, inspection robotics, and manufacturing automation.

Research Highlights

Adaptive Control

Controllers that tune themselves online to steer nonlinear systems with long delays, with stability proven mathematically (Lyapunov-based MRAC).

Multi-Physics Sensing

Three custom sensors (piezoelectric, triboelectric, optical) whose readings are fused into one reliable measurement, even under heavy noise.

Learning-Based Systems

Uncertainty estimates for robot foundation models (99.2% on the LIBERO benchmark), deep reinforcement learning for control, and teaching cheap sensors to imitate expensive ones via cross-modal knowledge transfer.

Autonomous FPV Drone Racing

SO(3) geometric control, one-shot gate detection, and an Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) for on-the-fly gate localization — a full autonomy stack for high-speed FPV drone racing.

Vision-Language Action Models

Confidence-guided dynamic action chunking for OpenVLA — a last-layer Jacobian L1-norm signal truncates unreliable actions, lifting LIBERO-SPATIAL success to 99.2%.

Detecting JWST Mirror Misalignment

A two-head Swin Transformer reads a single James Webb point-spread function and says which of the 18 mirror segments drifted and by how much — F1 = 1.0 on localization and 0.14–10.1% error on piston/tip/tilt.

Deformable Kernels for Curved-Surface NDE

Deformable convolution that bends to follow curved surfaces — maps material thickness across a full part instead of point by point.

Lyapunov-Based MRAC for Powder Flow

A Lyapunov-based MRAC controller that adapts to changing feeder dynamics and cancels periodic powder-flow surging — deployed on an FPGA with real stainless-steel powder.

Multi-Modal Powder-Flow Sensors

Three novel in-line sensors (piezoelectric, optical, triboelectric) benchmarked against an ultrasonic baseline for metal-3D-printing powder flow — all hit R² > 0.9 at a 90× lower data rate, then fused with a Kalman filter.

Automated 3D Point Cloud Registration

An automated pipeline that precisely aligns multi-view LiDAR point clouds with no manual intervention — FPFH features → RANSAC → ICP refinement.

Community STEM Outreach

STEM enrichment for underserved K-12 students in Atlanta.
